Solutions

How the hard-disk adaptations run from GEMDOS and GEMDRIVE, and what fixes the rest

This page gives every hard-disk adaptation on the 8bitchip.info website's list a verdict for a GEMDOS drive: a drive that serves files by name and has no disk sectors, such as Hatari's or the SidecarTridge Multidevice's GEMDRIVE. Of the 1,831 games, 1,608 run as they are, 212 need a fix, 1 is not feasible and 10 are unsure. Four solutions, S1 to S4, fix the 212. The page explains why each group fails, what each solution changes, and what has been built and tested.

Each game has one row in src/survey/migration.csv: its strategy, verdict, run, reason and solution. The strategies come from the games' files (recognising-modes.md). The run folders are under tools/dev/builds/survey/:

  • runs/: every game's run, one line each in runs.tsv;
  • trials/: the trials of the fixes;
  • runs-other.tsv: the runs of the games' other downloads.

Every verdict was reached on Hatari 2.6.1, with an ST and TOS 1.04 (an STE and TOS 1.62 for the STE games), from Hatari's GEMDOS drive. The working reference is the same game from an ACSI disk image booted by PPDRIVER, the hard-disk driver the adaptations look for.

1. In short

  • Two ways to the disk. The games follow twelve strategies (§2). Ten strategies, with 1,627 games, reach their files by name through the machine's GEMDOS. The other two, the Gamex launcher with GOS4 or GOS5 (204 games, Lemmings among them), read the disk by sectors through PPDRIVER. GOS is a TOS 1.04 GEMDOS that the launcher runs from RAM.
  • The verdicts. 1,608 as it is, 212 fix, 1 not feasible, 10 unsure.
  • The fixes. Four solutions cover the 212 games (§4):
    • S1 makes GOS read its files through the GEMDOS drive: the 204 GOS4 and GOS5 games.
    • S2 gives the GEMDOS drive a BPB, the block that describes a drive's layout: four Silmarils games.
    • S3 sets the fastload bit, a flag in a program's header that keeps its free memory from being cleared: three games whose start program Hatari ends when it loads the game.
    • S4 adds a missing start program: one game.
  • Where they stand. Trials showed S2, S3 and S4 (§3). S1 is built for the 87 GOS5 games and the 117 GOS4 games. 85 GOS5 games run from the GEMDOS drive, in Hatari and with the Multidevice; Awesome and Prison do not yet (gos5-gemdrive.md). The 117 GOS4 games run in Hatari, and those tried with the Multidevice run there too (gos4-gemdrive.md). S2, S3 and S4 are built too: seven of their eight games run, in Hatari and on the Multidevice; Aquanaut does not yet (small-fixes.md).
  • Tested twice. Every solution runs in Hatari's GEMDOS drive and then on real machines with the SidecarTridge Multidevice's GEMDRIVE. Only the exceptions are reported: what fails, and where the Multidevice differs from Hatari (§7).
  • Other downloads. 43 of the 204 GOS games have another download on the 8bitchip.info website that runs as it is from the GEMDOS drive (§5). Lemmings' own 2 MB RAM-disk version is one of them.

  • Every GOS4 and GOS5 game is "fix". From the GEMDOS drive on the ST, its launcher quits to the desktop or prints "This prg. requires PP, AHDI or Hddriver". The representative games do the same on the STE with TOS 1.62 and 2.06, and they play from PPDRIVER on all three machines.
  • Where the verdicts come from. 241 come from a human review of the runs, on the review page (review.tsv), 90 come from the game's PPDRIVER run, and 10 from a trace or a trial looked into (findings.tsv). The other 1,490 come from the run alone.

3. What the games to fix need

3.1 GOS4 and GOS5: the driver, then sectors (204 games)

Lemmings showed it first (gemdrive-failure.md, disk-layer.md). Every GOS4 and GOS5 launcher makes the same checks (launcher-signatures.md, "Other modes"). The launcher needs the driver in two steps:

  1. The driver. The launcher looks for a resident PPDRIVER: the mark PPHD just before the routine that hdv_bpb points to. If it finds it, it copies 7,204 bytes of the driver to $B7180 and patches them. If not, it reads sector 0 itself, then gives up. It reads through the ACSI registers on TOS 1, with DMAread on pun_ptr's device on TOS 2, and through IDE on a Falcon.
  2. The sectors. GOS (D15R, TOS 1.04 running from RAM) then serves the game's file requests with its own GEMDOS. That GEMDOS reads the FAT volume by sectors through the driver copy, with Getbpb, Mediach and Rwabs on drive C. The two GOS versions differ in who calls it. GOS5's file_io asks for whole files by name: Fopen, Fseek, Fread or Fwrite, Fclose (disk-layer.md §2). In GOS4, the game runs under GOS's GEMDOS and calls it itself.

A GEMDOS drive has no driver and no sectors, so both steps fail. The runs, each with its PPDRIVER and STE runs beside it:

  • Lemmings, runs/20261011-210525-st-104-gemdrive-695-lemmings: the launcher quits;
  • Operation Stealth (GOS4), runs/20261012-012833-st-104-gemdrive-1195-operation-stealth;
  • Thunderhawk AH-73M (GOS5), runs/20261012-094035-st-104-gemdrive-1476-thunderhawk-ah-73m.

The 204 games carry 27 builds of GOS. 13 are for the ST and STE: D15R_HH4.FIC (5 builds, in 117 games), D15R_5H.FIC (1 build, in 56), D15R_H5.FIC (5 builds, in 31) and D15R_H15.FIC (2 builds, in 3). The other 14 are for the Falcon (D15R_F4, _5F, _F5, _F15).

3.2 Getbpb on C: (4 games)

A BPB (BIOS parameter block) tells TOS the layout of a drive: its sector and cluster sizes, and where its FAT and directory are. Crystals of Arborea, Targhan (one download, ARBOTARG.ZIP), Robinsons Requiem and Storm Master are all Silmarils games, and they start with the same kind of START.PRG. Before every file, that program calls Dgetdrv, then BIOS Getbpb on that drive. Once it gets a BPB, it opens the file through GEMDOS. From PPDRIVER, for example, it calls Getbpb(0x2) at $1C21A, then Fopen("MAIN.AO") (runs/20261012-135728-st-104-ppdriver-1698-crystals-of-arborea).

A GEMDOS drive has no BPB. TOS answers 0, and the program asks again, millions of times (runs/20261012-103727-st-104-gemdrive-1698-crystals-of-arborea). The programs do not read sectors: once given a BPB, they read everything by name.

Trial. At the program's first instruction, the debugger writes a 22-byte routine at $380 and points hdv_bpb at it. For drive 2, the routine returns PPDRIVER's BPB, stored at $3A0. For any other drive, it goes on to TOS's routine at $FC173C. From the GEMDOS drive, the four games then run, with no Rwabs on C:

3.3 A start program whose stack is cleared (3 games)

Spy vs Spy 2, Strike Force Harrier and Aquanaut each have a start program that starts the game with Pexec: Pexec(0, "SPY2.BOY"), HARRIER.TOS and AQUANAUT.EXE. Before that, the start program shrinks its memory to its own code with Mshrink. Its stack stays at the top of RAM, in the memory it gave back. From the GEMDOS drive, the game never runs and the desktop comes back.

The cause is in the way Hatari's GEMDOS drive loads a program. Hatari loads the program itself. Unless the program's header has the fastload bit, Hatari clears the new program's free memory (../hatari/src/gemdos.c:4383-4395), and the caller's stack with it. The fastload bit is bit 0 of byte 25 of the header. Hatari then rewrites only the mode and the basepage of the saved Pexec call (gemdos.c:4504-4506). The function number is now 0, so TOS reads the call as Pterm0 and ends the start program. The stack before and after the load is 00 4b 0000 000125e4 000125e0 → 00 00 0006 00000000 00012766 (trials/spy2/runs/20261013-112912-st-104-gemdrive-spy2-usp). From PPDRIVER, TOS's own Pexec reads its arguments before it clears the memory, and the games run.

Trial. With the fastload bit set in the started program, the three games get past the start from the GEMDOS drive:

  • Spy vs Spy 2 plays its demo (trials/spy2/runs/20261013-112748-st-104-gemdrive-spy2-fastload);
  • Strike Force Harrier reaches its simulation menu (trials/sfh/runs/20261013-112833-st-104-gemdrive-sfh-fastload);
  • Aquanaut reaches its title (trials/aquanaut/runs/20261013-113519-st-104-gemdrive-aquanaut-fastload).

No other GEMDOS-drive run shows a program loaded and its caller ended at once.

3.4 Rody and Mastico 2 (1 game)

Its download has its HAGE module, its floppy image and T14AG.FIC, but no RUNME.TOS. HAGE is the launcher module that loads TOS 1.04 with GEM into RAM and reads the game's floppy from an image file. Rody and Mastico 4's download (RODYM4.ZIP) has the same layout. With its RUNME.TOS, Rody and Mastico 2 reaches its title and chapter menu from the GEMDOS drive (trials/rodym2/).

3.5 Not feasible: Great Giana Sisters STE (1 game)

During play, the game streams its music from GS.RAW by sectors. GIAACSI.TOS finds the file's sectors through the driver's PUN_INFO, then reads them itself with the ACSI or IDE registers. Its README asks for a driver "which supports PUN info" and for defragmented audio files. From the GEMDOS drive it prints "Error in getting file location" and quits (runs/20261012-132330-ste-162-gemdrive-1112-great-giana-sisters-ste).

The game's page also has two HAGA downloads, GIANAA.ZIP and GIANAS.ZIP, and they do the same: the error from the GEMDOS drive, the intro from PPDRIVER (runs-other.tsv). Running the game would take a new music streamer that reads through GEMDOS. That is a change to the game, not to its launcher: the "major modification" that makes a game not feasible.

3.6 Unsure (10 games)

Nine games run "as it is" by the rules, but the human review marked their runs Broken on the review page. A second look, with the PPDRIVER run beside each, kept them unsure. The tenth game has no hard-disk version.

S1. GOS reads through GEMDOS and GEMDRIVE (204 games)

The problem. The launcher wants PPDRIVER, and GOS reads drive C by sectors (§3.1).

The idea. GOS keeps everything but its disk access. Its file requests go to the TRAP #1 handler, the GEMDOS entry, that the machine had before the launcher ran. The HAGA adaptations' file service has worked this way since 2012 (modes.md §3.1). It takes a name, an offset, a length, a buffer and a mode, calls the machine's GEMDOS, then puts the game's state back. 1,361 of the 1,366 games of the HAGA family run as they are. GOS5's file_io takes the same parameters.

In Hatari, that handler is the cartridge hook at $FA002A, which serves the drive from the host folder. It takes its parameters from the stack (gemdos.c:4028-4038) and copies Fread's data straight into the buffer (gemdos.c:2364-2365). None of TOS's RAM is in the way.

The proposal. Two changes, both on the Atari side:

  1. The launcher's driver checks. Each GOS launcher (START.TOS, packed) checks for PPHD with the same code, 2278 0472 0CA9 5050 4844 FFF8, in all 204. 203 of them also have the DMAread fallback. The patch makes the launcher go on without a driver: no driver copy, no sector-0 read, and GOS started as on the launcher's main path. The launcher is unpacked and packed again with tools/unpack.py and tools/pack.py, which make a packed file the launcher accepts (launcher-formats.md). Lemmings' launcher sources, which reassemble to the original bytes, show the place: src/lemld2/lemld2.s, at check_pp_driver, reuse_pp_driver and start_d15r.
  2. GOS's GEMDOS entry. Each D15R build has its own GEMDOS entry, trap1 (302F 0006 B07C 000B …). The patch makes it pass the file functions to the handler saved before the launcher ran: Fopen, Fclose, Fread, Fwrite, Fseek, Fcreate, Fdelete, Fsfirst/Fsnext, Dsetdrv, Dsetpath and Dgetpath. There are 13 ST/STE builds to patch (§3.1). The new code goes where D15R's own code sits, at the start of each build (1,852 bytes in D15R_H5).
  • Work. The two patchers, which find their place by signature as classify.py does. A script that applies them to a copy of a game. Then a run of the 204 games with run_all.py. Lemmings comes first: its launcher and GOS are rebuilt from sources that reassemble to the original bytes, with their round trips and tests. The Falcon builds take the same patch, untested: the Falcon is out of scope.
  • Memory. None beyond GOS's own. The driver copy at $B7180 and its buffers go unused.
  • Risks.
    • GOS4 games call GOS's GEMDOS themselves during play. Hatari's trace does not show these calls, because it sees only the calls that reach its hook. Logged at GOS's own dispatcher, they are the file calls, Pexec to start programs, and Fsfirst to search. The GOS4 build passes on all three (gos4-gemdrive.md).
    • Fsfirst and Fsnext find the DTA through TOS's current process (gemdos.c:3043-3049). A game that owns low RAM may have overwritten it.
    • Gamex's state saves (file_io with "Wr") are untested.
    • From PPDRIVER, the reference, the original launcher stops at Lemmings' first level in Hatari, on an address error. The cause is a keyboard packet cut in two by a disk load. The S1 build lets a packet under way finish before each load (gos5-gemdrive.md), and Lemmings plays its first level from the GEMDOS drive, in Hatari and with the Multidevice. The original launcher is left as it is.
  • SidecarTridge. Its GEMDRIVE also hooks TRAP #1 from cartridge ROM. But it reads _longframe ($59E) and runs a wait loop in RAM at _dskbufp ($4C6, or on the stack). It also calls TRAP #1 again through $84 for paths without a drive letter and for Fsfirst/Fsnext (gemdrive.s:244-266, 811-815, 1100-1111, 1344-1349). With GOS's image of TOS's RAM in place, those variables and vectors must hold usable values. The requests should also carry full paths (C:\…).

Built for GOS5 (gos5-gemdrive.md). The build changes the launcher only. tools/build_gos5.py finds each launcher by signature, in all 87. It puts a block of code (src/gemdrive/gos5_patch.s) in front of the launcher's text, and turns the PPHD check into a branch to that block. GOS's code stays unchanged. GOS keeps its own image of the system variables, and the block points the TRAP #1 vector of that image at a forwarder. The forwarder is a short routine that passes the file functions to the machine's handler, with names cut to 8.3, and every other call to GOS. On the Multidevice, the block also moves GEMDRIVE's stack out of the game's memory. Before each load, it lets a keyboard message under way finish.

The result: 85 games run. Five of them need a disk in drive A, because they wait for the floppy motor themselves. Forgotten Worlds needs two GOS files from another download, Magic Boy needs 2 MB and Muzzy an STE. Awesome and Prison still fail. Each refuses the cartridge in the port, and two bytes of one of its files turn that check off.

Built for GOS4 (gos4-gemdrive.md). The change is of the same kind. tools/build_gos4.py finds each launcher by signature, in all 117, and adds its own block (src/gemdrive/gos4_patch.s). GOS4 is TOS 1.04 started from RAM, and it sets up its own TRAP #1. The block points that vector, in GOS's copy in memory, at a forwarder. The forwarder:

  • passes the file calls to the machine's handler, with names cut to 8.3 and file handles numbered as GOS numbers its own;
  • reads the programs that are started with Pexec through the drive;
  • gives Fsfirst the game's DTA;
  • leaves drives A and B to GOS's floppy code, and gives drive C a BPB.

All 117 run in Hatari. F16 Falcon needs 2 MB. Asterix and Winter Games also read drive A, as they do with PPDRIVER.

Not proposed, for the same 204 games:

  • A driver that serves sectors from a disk image. A resident driver shaped like PPDRIVER would answer Rwabs from a disk-image file on the GEMDOS drive. It would carry the PPHD mark and follow the layout at the offsets the launcher patches (gemdrive-failure.md §5). Its code would have to fit in the 7,204 bytes the launcher copies. The original files would stay unchanged, but the games would live in an image, which Hatari's ACSI image already gives.
  • A driver that makes a FAT volume out of the folder. The same driver, with the directory and FAT sectors built from Fsfirst/Fsnext, so that the files stay loose in the folder. It is the most work, under the same 7,204-byte limit.
  • The games' other downloads. No work, but only for the 43 games that have one (§5).

S2. A BPB for GEMDOS and GEMDRIVE (4 games)

The problem. The four Silmarils programs wait for a BPB that a GEMDOS drive does not give (§3.2).

The fix. A resident program, run from C:\AUTO\, hooks hdv_bpb with an XBRA header. For the GEMDOS drive's number, it returns a BPB. For any other drive, it calls the routine that was there before. The trial's routine (§3.2) is the core of that program: 22 bytes and an 18-byte BPB.

  • Work. Small.
  • Memory. About 100 bytes, resident.
  • Risk. A program that reads C: by sectors once it has a BPB would get TOS's error. None of the four did.
  • SidecarTridge. Its GEMDRIVE has no hdv_* hooks either (none in gemdrive.s), so the same program applies there.

Built. GDBPB.PRG is 82 bytes in memory and answers for any drive from C: up that has no BPB of its own. It is run once before the game. The four games run in Hatari and on the Multidevice; Storm Master also needs a floppy in drive A (small-fixes.md).

S3. The fastload bit (3 games)

The problem. Hatari clears the started program's free memory, and the start program's stack with it (§3.3).

The fix. Set bit 0 of byte 25 of the header in SPY2.BOY, HARRIER.TOS and AQUANAUT.EXE. Hatari then leaves their free memory as it is, with the caller's stack in it.

  • Work. One byte per game. Another way is to move the start programs' stack into the memory they keep. Their sources are in the downloads (SPY22.S, START1.S).
  • Risk. A program that counts on zeroed memory. None of the three showed it.
  • Hatari. The cause is Hatari's: GemDOS_PexecBpCreated relies on the caller's stack after it clears the free memory. That calls for a report upstream, not a change here: Hatari stays as it is.
  • SidecarTridge. Its GEMDRIVE runs Pexec modes 0 and 3 itself (gemdrive.s:1412-1415). Whether it clears memory the same way is not checked.

Built. tools/set_fastload.py sets the bit. Spy vs Spy 2 and Strike Force Harrier then play, in Hatari and on the Multidevice. Aquanaut reaches its title in Hatari but reboots on the Multidevice; it is not solved yet (small-fixes.md).

S4. Rody and Mastico 4's RUNME.TOS (1 game)

The problem. Rody and Mastico 2's download has no start program (§3.4).

The fix. Copy RUNME.TOS from RODYM4.ZIP into Rody and Mastico 2's folder. The trial already showed it works.

Built. tools/rody2_runme.py makes the copy. The game reaches its chapter menu in Hatari and runs on the Multidevice (small-fixes.md).

5. The GOS games' other downloads

43 of the 204 GOS games have another download whose strategy reads by name (the other_downloads column). tools/survey/run_other.py ran each one from the GEMDOS drive, with run_all.py's rules. All 43 run as they are (runs-other.tsv, with the snapshots in snapshots-other/). Every snapshot was looked at: each shows a title, a menu, the attract mode or play. The 43 are:

  • 33 GOS5 games with a HAGA download, most of them "done by JY" (…JY.ZIP);
  • five GOS4 games: Strider 2, Axel's Magic Hammer and Street Fighter (HAGA), and Alien Syndrome and Strider (HAG2);
  • Lemmings' LEMMSRD.ZIP (FWRD RAM disk, 2 MB), which reaches its title (runs/20261013-112842-st-104-gemdrive-695-lemmsrd-lemmings);
  • Magic Boy's MAGICBRD.ZIP (Gamex);
  • Corruption, Fish and Guild of Thieves's COMP.ZIP (plain). It must be in C:\COMP, as its README asks. In C:\GAME it stops with "No System Resources".

In migration.csv, the solution column names the other download after S1: "or … runs as it is".

6. Corrections made on the way

  • Weird Dreams. Its first run counted "418 sector reads on C:". They were reads of its own RAM disk, drive M: (device 12), on both disks, and the two runs end on the same title. 7. How the solutions are tested

Every game was run on the first bench, the Hatari emulator's GEMDOS drive. Every solution is then tried on the second, real Atari machines with the SidecarTridge Multidevice's GEMDRIVE, before it counts. The real machines find failures that Hatari does not show, and each one is reported here and in the catalogue.

7.1 The two benches

Hatari's GEMDOS driveThe Multidevice's GEMDRIVE

Both serve files by name and have no disk sectors, so a game that runs on one should run on the other. They differ in what surrounds the drive: GEMDRIVE runs on the Atari itself, from cartridge ROM, and uses some of the Atari's RAM; real machines have real floppy drives, real timing and the memory left over by what ran before.

7.2 How a game is tried on the Multidevice

  • The test card holds every game in a folder named by its verdict and RAM: PPHD\<verdict>\<RAM>\<group>\<game>\, with INDEX.TXT giving each game's program to start. FIXGOS5, FIXGOS4, FIXPLAIN, FIXHOLE and FIXHAGE hold the fixed games, STILLNOT the fixed games that still fail.
  • The card is written by tools/survey/sdcard.py and checked file by file against the build.
  • The machine is switched off and on between games. After a reset, memory and hooked vectors are left over from the game before, and some games fail. A game that fails after a reset is tried again after a power cycle before the failure counts.

7.3 What is reported

Hatari's GEMDOS drive and the Multidevice's GEMDRIVE behave the same on every machine. A game runs on any machine and from both drives unless it is reported in this section, so only the exceptions are listed: where the Multidevice differs from Hatari, and what fails. Every fix was tried on the Multidevice: the GOS5 and GOS4 builds and the small fixes, and a sample of the games that run as they are.

7.4 Where the Multidevice differs from Hatari

  • GEMDRIVE's stack in the game's memory (the GOS games). GEMDRIVE runs the calls it serves on a 1 KB stack that it reserves at boot, at the bottom of free memory. A GOS game uses that memory for itself. Found by reading GEMDRIVE's source and reproducing its memory use in Hatari, where the game then stopped, before the first card test. The builds move GEMDRIVE's stack into memory the game leaves alone (gos5-gemdrive.md).
  • A keyboard packet cut by a load (Lemmings). On the STE, pressing space during the intro hung the game. A disk load had cut a mouse packet from the keyboard in two, and the game then read every packet out of step. Hatari showed the same cause as an address error at the first level. The builds let a packet under way finish before each load.
  • A file locked by GEMDRIVE (Noddy's Playtime). The game opens a file for reading and writing, never closes it, and later loads it again. GOS and Hatari allow that. GEMDRIVE keeps its files in FatFs, which refuses to open again a file that is open for writing, and that cannot be changed. The game took the refusal for a missing disk and asked for disk 1 forever. The GOS4 build now opens such a file for reading only (gos4-gemdrive.md).
  • Free memory that starts higher (Aquanaut). The game reboots the machine while it loads. It runs only at the address where a bare TOS 1.04 loads it, and with GEMDRIVE free memory starts higher. Not solved (small-fixes.md).
  • The floppy drive (Chaos Engine). On the STE the game waits for the floppy motor. With any floppy in drive A it goes on.
  • What a reset leaves behind (3D Construction Kit). Three bombs after other games and a reset; it runs after a power cycle.

7.5 Failures on both benches

  • Awesome and Prison refuse a cartridge in the port, and both GEMDRIVEs need one there (gos5-gemdrive.md).
  • Storm Master drives the floppy controller itself at its credits and waits for a disk in drive A. It does the same from a hard disk with PPDRIVER.
  • The ten unsure games fail in Hatari and on the Multidevice; why is still to be found.

7.6 How a failure is reported

A failure on the Multidevice is recorded with what was seen and the machine, in src/survey/hardware.tsv. The catalogue shows it in the game's row, and this section lists its cause once it is known. A game that fails only on the Multidevice is reported even when it runs in Hatari.

Tools

  • tools/survey/run_other.py: the other downloads (§5).
  • src/survey/solutions.tsv: each solution, by row or by strategy. verdicts.py writes it into migration.csv's solution column, with the other downloads that run as they are.
  • src/survey/findings.tsv: the verdicts settled by a trace or a trial, with their runs.

The GOS5 games from GEMDOS and GEMDRIVE

87 games on the 8bitchip.info website start from a hard disk with the Gamex launcher and GOS5. As they come, they do not start from a GEMDOS drive, a drive that serves files by name and has no disk sectors: Hatari's, or the SidecarTridge Multidevice's GEMDRIVE. tools/build_gos5.py rebuilds their start program so that they do. 85 of the 87 run that way, in Hatari and with the Multidevice.

GOS5 is a GEMDOS 0.15 that the launcher runs from RAM, and it serves the game's file requests. It reads the hard disk by sectors through PPDRIVER, a hard-disk driver that must be resident. A GEMDOS drive has neither the driver nor the sectors, so the launcher gives up (solutions.md §3.1).

What the build changes

The build changes only the game's start program: START.TOS, or the one its folder starts, such as Chaos Engine's START2M.TOS. The game's data and the adaptation's other files stay as they are.

  • The Gamex launcher still runs: its info screen, its cheat keys, the adaptation's cover picture, and GOS.
  • With PPDRIVER present, the launcher runs unchanged.
  • Without PPDRIVER, if a GEMDOS drive answers TRAP #1 from the cartridge port (Hatari's or the Multidevice's), GOS's file requests go to that drive by name instead of reading sectors. Each name is cut to 8.3, as TOS does.
  • On the Multidevice, the build also moves GEMDRIVE's stack out of the game's memory.
  • Before each load, the build lets a keyboard message already under way finish, so that the load does not cut it in two.
  • The info screen says "Patched for GEMDRIVE HD by SidecarTridge". For the games below that need a floppy, it also says "Insert any floppy disk in drive A".

Building a game

You need this repository and Python 3. The first time, you also need the toolchain described in tools/dev/README.md: the tool assembles the change once, into tools/dev/builds/gos5/.

  1. Unpack the game's download from the 8bitchip.info website. Keep the original start program aside as START.ORG. The start program is START.TOS in every game but Chaos Engine, where it is START2M.TOS.
  2. Build: 
    python3 tools/build_gos5.py GAME/START.ORG GAME/START.TOS

    For the games below that need a disk in drive A, add --floppy. The tool checks the original by its signature. It refuses a start program it does not know, or one already built, and then writes nothing. It prints the SHA-256 of both files.

  3. Copy the game's folder to the GEMDOS drive and start START.TOS from it.

To undo, put START.ORG back as START.TOS.

The GOS4 games from GEMDOS and GEMDRIVE

117 games on the 8bitchip.info website start from a hard disk with the Gamex launcher and GOS4. As they come, they do not start from a GEMDOS drive, a drive that serves files by name and has no disk sectors: Hatari's, or the SidecarTridge Multidevice's GEMDRIVE. tools/build_gos4.py rebuilds their start program so that they do. All 117 run that way in Hatari, and the ten tried with the Multidevice run there too.

GOS4 is TOS 1.04 that the launcher starts from RAM, and the game itself calls its GEMDOS while it runs. GOS reads the hard disk by sectors through PPDRIVER, a hard-disk driver that must be resident. A GEMDOS drive has neither the driver nor the sectors (solutions.md §3.1).

What the build changes

The build changes only the game's start program (START.TOS). The game's data, GOS (D15R_HH4.FIC) and the other files stay as they are.

  • The Gamex launcher still runs: its info screen, its keys, the cover picture, and GOS.
  • With PPDRIVER present, the launcher runs as it was made.
  • Without PPDRIVER, if a GEMDOS drive answers TRAP #1 from the cartridge port, the launcher installs a forwarder before GOS starts. A forwarder is a short routine that passes each call on. GOS sets up its own TRAP #1, and the launcher points it, in GOS's copy in memory, at the forwarder. The forwarder passes the file calls to the GEMDOS drive by name, and every other call to GOS (below).
  • On the Multidevice, the build also moves GEMDRIVE's stack out of the game's memory.
  • The info screen says "Patched for GEMDRIVE HD by SidecarTridge".

What the forwarder does

  • Names. Each part of a name is cut to 8.3, as TOS does. An empty name gets the answer GOS would give.
  • File handles. The drive's file handles are numbered as GOS numbers its own, 6 and up. Some adaptations put back a snapshot of a game's variables that holds such a number, as Operation Stealth's does.
  • Programs. A program that the launcher or the game starts with Pexec is read through the drive into GOS's memory. GOS then runs it.
  • Files opened for reading and writing. The forwarder opens them on the drive for reading only. The Multidevice's GEMDRIVE refuses to open a file again while it is open for writing. Noddy's Playtime opens data\driving.dat for reading and writing, never closes it, and then loads it. On the Multidevice, it asked for disk 1 forever. No game writes through such a file: the files the games write, they create.
  • Searches. Fsfirst and Fsnext fill the game's DTA.
  • Drives. Drives A and B stay with GOS's floppy code. Drive C gives a BPB, the block that describes a drive's layout, to the games that ask for one (Starblade, Windsurf Willy). It reads no sector.

A BPB for GEMDOS and GEMDRIVE: GDBPB.PRG

A BPB (BIOS parameter block) tells TOS the layout of a drive. The four Silmarils games start with a START.PRG that asks BIOS Getbpb for the current drive's BPB before each file it opens. Once it gets one, it opens the file by name. A GEMDOS drive has no BPB, so TOS answers with none. The program then asks again forever, and the screen stays still.

GDBPB.PRG stays in memory, in 82 bytes. It answers for any drive from C: up that has no BPB of its own, so the GEMDOS drive's letter does not matter. Floppies, and drives that a hard-disk driver serves, keep their own answers. It is built from src/gemdrive/gdbpb.s. Run it once after each reset or power-on, before the game. It prints "GEMDOS drive BPB installed", or "already installed" when run again. A reset removes it.

Storm Master also reads the floppy drive itself at its credits, without TOS, and waits there for an answer. Put any floppy in drive A. The game does the same from a hard disk with the PPDRIVER driver.

The fastload bit: tools/set_fastload.py

The fastload bit is bit 0 of byte 25 of a program's header. When it is set, the loader leaves the program's free memory as it is instead of clearing it.

The start programs of Spy vs Spy 2, Strike Force Harrier and Aquanaut give their memory back with their stack still in it, then start the game. Hatari's GEMDOS drive clears the free memory of a program it loads, unless the program has the fastload bit, and the start program's stack is cleared with it. The start program is then ended as soon as the game is loaded, and the desktop comes back. tools/set_fastload.py sets the bit in the program that the start program loads.

Rody and Mastico 2's start program: tools/rody2_runme.py

Rody and Mastico 2's download has the game's HAGE module, the floppy image and the data, but no RUNME.TOS. HAGE is the launcher module that loads TOS 1.04 with GEM into RAM and reads the game's floppy from an image file. Rody and Mastico 4's download has the same HAGE, so its RUNME.TOS starts Rody and Mastico 2 too. The game's chapter menu takes the mouse only once its music has finished.

Aquanaut

With the fastload bit set, Aquanaut reaches its title in Hatari: on an ST with TOS 1.04, and on an STE with TOS 1.62 or 2.06. On TOS 2.06, its title's credit lines are garbled. On the STE with the Multidevice, it reboots while it loads. What is known so far:

  • AQUANAUT.PRG starts AQUANAUT.EXE right above itself in memory, and both put the screen at fixed addresses.
  • When the free memory starts 64 KB higher than where Hatari's GEMDOS drive leaves it, the game fails in Hatari too.
  • The 8bitchip.info list marks the game "Hole" on TOS 2.06, the Mega STE and the Falcon. Its download's FBHOLE.PRG writes a boot floppy that keeps the memory below 512 KB free at power-on. With that memory kept free, the game is loaded above 512 KB, and it still fails in Hatari.

It is not solved yet.

Tested

  • Hatari, on an ST with TOS 1.04 and an STE with TOS 1.62 and 2.06, from its GEMDOS drive:
    • the four Silmarils games play or reach their title, with no sector read on C:. Without GDBPB.PRG, Crystals of Arborea asks for the BPB 6.4 million times in a run and opens nothing;
    • Spy vs Spy 2 and Strike Force Harrier play;
    • Rody and Mastico 2 reaches its chapter menu;
    • from the PPDRIVER driver, the fastload bit changes nothing;
    • a game that needs no BPB (Stormlord) runs with GDBPB.PRG in memory.
